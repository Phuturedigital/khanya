The idea behind Khanya

A brand invented to answer one question: what would a South African dental practice look like online if it led with calm and candour instead of stock-photo perfection?

The name

Khanya means to shine

Khanya is a verb in isiXhosa and isiZulu: to shine, to give light, to become bright. It is also a common given name across South Africa.

Why it fits

Dentistry is quiet, technical and largely invisible work. Nobody admires a well-placed filling. What people actually notice is what happens afterwards — someone stops covering their mouth when they laugh. That is the light, and it belongs to the patient rather than the practice.

Why not a surname

Most South African dental practices are named after the principal dentist. That works until the dentist retires or sells. A verb outlasts a person, and it says something about the outcome rather than about who owns the building.

Design

Taken from the mark, not invented

The palette is read straight out of the supplied Khanya logo. The navy and teal are declared fills in the source file; the gold sits inside an embedded raster and was sampled from the arc itself.

Typography

Archivo sets every heading in tight uppercase — a grotesque with enough width to hold at display size without turning into a condensed poster face. Inter carries body copy at a light weight, which keeps long clinical explanations from feeling heavy.

Both are open-licensed and served from Google Fonts, so there is no licensing cost to carry into production — unlike the licensed display faces most brand packs specify.

The mark

Redrawn as clean vector from the supplied logo design. The originals wrap embedded raster images and their artwork overflows the declared canvas, so the tooth is clipped flat at its base and the wordmark survives only as a “K”. This redraw keeps the design — brush-stroke tooth, open gold arc, sparkle — as real paths that scale and recolour.

Honest status

What this is, and what it is not

What is real

  • The design, the build and the code. Seven static pages, no framework.
  • The typography, palette and layout system.
  • The responsive behaviour, at 1440 and at 390.
  • Phuture Digital, who made it.

What is invented

  • The brand. There is no Khanya Dental Studio.
  • Every practitioner. The photographs are licensed stock models.
  • Every price, statistic and review on the site.
  • The Sea Point address and the contact details.
No HPCSA registration exists for this practice. Dentistry in South Africa is regulated by the Health Professions Council of South Africa, and a real practice would display its practice number and its practitioners' registration details. This one displays none, because there are none. Do not use this site to make any decision about dental care.
